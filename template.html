The problem

In winter, the light in your home depends on the water in one reservoir

But an ordinary person has no easy way to know how much water is there: official figures appear rarely and are hard to compare with anything. Is it a lot or a little? Will it last the winter?

Summer fills it, winter empties it

Snow and glaciers melt in summer and the reservoir rises. In the cold months the rivers run low, heating pushes demand up, and the power plants live on what was saved.

Now look at the real thing, from space

How to read this picture

  • It is not a photo. The satellite sends radio waves to the ground and records their echo.
  • Black is water. A smooth surface bounces the signal away like a mirror, so almost nothing comes back.
  • Grey and white is land. Rough ground, mountains and buildings send the signal back.
  • The blue line is the shoreline our program found. Orange marks water that was not there on the first date.
  • Pale vertical stripes on the water come from the radar itself. They are not waves.

Why saving electricity at home helps

Every kilowatt-hour not used is water that stays behind the dam until spring. As the president told citizens in November 2025, every light bulb left on costs Toktogul hundreds of litres of water.

Source: 24.kg, 10 November 2025

Will there be limits on electricity?

In an interview published on 11 September 2026, the Minister of Energy said the ministry does not plan restrictions on electricity use this winter, including a 5 kW limit for households. How the winter goes depends on the weather, on imports and on how much electricity all of us use.

How we tell water from dry ground

For this radar, calm water and dry, smooth ground are both very dark. One image cannot tell them apart. Rain can: wet ground turns bright, and water does not change. Drag the divider to compare a dry day with a wet one.

On the dry day the plain in the lower right is almost as dark as the reservoir. On the wet day it is clearly brighter, and the reservoir is not. A brightness threshold on one image counted such ground as water: our first attempt gave about 700 km² instead of about 215.

    We tested it

    Run on simulated radar scenes where the right answer is known in advance, the same code came within 1 km² of the true area. Real ground is messier.

    What this page cannot tell you

      Short answers

      Is this a forecast of power cuts?

      No. The page shows how much water surface the radar sees and what officials have said about the volume. Whether limits are introduced is decided by the government and depends on the weather, on imports and on how much electricity people use.

      Why do you show area and not volume?

      The radar sees the surface, not the depth. The area tells us whether the reservoir is growing or shrinking. The volume in billions of cubic metres comes from official statements.
